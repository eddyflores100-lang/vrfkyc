# Human-first positioning and offers

VRFKYC is presented first as a review workspace for people making commercial decisions. Developer and agent integrations remain a separate technical layer. The English interface targets the U.S. audience; product claims reflect beta capabilities.

| Audience | Entry point | Offer available now | Live offer boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Individuals | A simple person/company example | Free fictional review, notes, local PDF/CSV/JSON/HTML export | A single actual-name review requires configured live access; no paid report checkout |
| Businesses | Supplier/commercial-partner review workflow | Company example and downloadable pilot-planning brief | Scope and provider access must be agreed; no shared accounts, bulk uploads, monitoring or persistent audit archive |
| Developers | Dedicated API/MCP sandbox | Public discovery, demo calls, schemas, resources, prompt and integration guide | Authorized live calls require backend configuration, token and appropriate provider terms |

The hero avoids API/MCP terminology. Human offers precede the developer console. Authorized settings are collapsed in the main form. Audience entry buttons choose the appropriate fictional fixture and reset to demo mode. Report field names remain stable for existing integrations.

Pilot and integration briefs are generated locally as text downloads. They collect only purpose, expected volume and requested scope; they do not submit a request, create an order or send a message. No price, delivery promise, customer reference or certification is invented. GitHub contact links carry a reminder not to publish private subject details.

Future commercial packaging could use single reviews for occasional users, scoped recurring access for businesses, and metered integrations for developers. These are packaging directions to validate with customers, not active products or approved prices. Provider costs, licensed resale rights, support scope and verified business terms must be settled before sale.
